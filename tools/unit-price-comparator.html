Frequently Asked Questions

What is unit price?

Unit price is the cost per single unit of measurement (like per gram, per liter, or per piece), calculated by dividing total price by total quantity.

Why compare unit price instead of total price?

Products come in different package sizes, so comparing total prices alone can be misleading — a bigger package costing more might actually be cheaper per unit.

Can I compare more than 2 products?

Yes, add as many products as you want to compare using the "Add Product" button.

Why Unit Price Matters More Than Total Price

Products are frequently sold in different package sizes, which makes comparing raw prices misleading. A 1kg bag at $2.50 and a 500g bag at $1.50 have very different total prices, but calculating the unit price reveals the 1kg bag actually costs less per gram ($0.0025/g vs $0.003/g) — making it the better deal despite the higher upfront cost. This kind of comparison is exactly what unit pricing labels on grocery store shelves are designed to help with, and this tool does the same calculation for any products you enter.

How the Unit Price Calculation Works

For each product, the tool divides the price you entered by the quantity you entered, producing a straightforward price-per-unit figure (price ÷ quantity = unit price). It then sorts every product from lowest to highest unit price and marks the cheapest one as the best value, while showing how much more expensive each other option is as a percentage relative to that cheapest price.

Matching Units Across Products

This comparison only gives an accurate result when every product's quantity is entered in the same unit of measurement. If one product is listed in grams and another in kilograms, convert them to a common unit first (for example, 1kg becomes 1000g) before entering the values, otherwise the calculated unit prices won't be comparable. A unit converter tool can help with this conversion step if the packaging labels use different units.

Why Package Size Tricks Can Mislead Shoppers

Retailers sometimes reduce a package's size while keeping the price the same — a practice often called "shrinkflation" — which makes the per-unit cost increase even though the shelf price looks unchanged. Because this tool calculates true cost per unit rather than relying on sticker price alone, it catches these size reductions that a quick glance at two price tags would miss.

Common Mistakes When Comparing Unit Prices

  • Mixing units — entering one product's quantity in ounces and another's in grams produces a meaningless comparison.
  • Ignoring quality differences — the cheapest unit price isn't always the better overall choice if the products differ in quality, concentration, or ingredients.
  • Forgetting bundled discounts — a "buy two, get one free" deal changes the effective price per unit and should be factored into the price you enter.
