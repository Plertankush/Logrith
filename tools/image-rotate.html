About the Image Rotate & Flip Tool

The Logrith Image Rotate & Flip tool is a free, browser-based utility for correcting image orientation and mirroring photos without installing any software. Whether you've got a sideways phone photo, a scanned document that came out upside down, or you simply need a mirrored version of an image for a design project, this tool handles it in a couple of clicks. All processing happens using the HTML5 canvas element directly in your browser, which means your image is never uploaded to a server — it stays private on your device from start to finish.

Rotating and flipping are two different operations that are often confused. Rotating turns the entire image around a central point, changing its orientation by a fixed angle — 90°, 180°, or 270°. Flipping, on the other hand, creates a mirror image along either the horizontal or vertical axis without changing the angle of the content itself. This tool supports both, so you can fix orientation issues and create mirrored versions from the same interface.

Common Reasons to Rotate or Flip an Image

  • Phone photos taken sideways. Photos captured while holding a phone in an unusual orientation sometimes save with incorrect rotation metadata, making them appear sideways or upside down in certain apps.
  • Scanned documents and receipts. Flatbed and sheet-fed scanners frequently produce pages that are rotated 90° or upside down depending on how the paper was fed in.
  • Social media and website graphics. Design templates sometimes call for a mirrored version of a photo or icon to balance a layout visually.
  • Screenshots from rotated displays. Screenshots taken on a monitor or device set to a non-standard orientation can come out rotated and need correcting before sharing.
  • Printing and framing. Getting the orientation right before printing avoids wasted paper or having to crop and rotate after the fact.

How Rotation and Flipping Work Behind the Scenes

When you click a rotate button, the tool draws your image onto a new HTML5 canvas using a transformation matrix that rotates the pixel grid by the chosen angle. For 90° and 270° rotations, the width and height of the canvas swap, since turning a landscape image on its side makes it portrait, and vice versa. For 180° rotation, the dimensions stay the same but every pixel is repositioned to the opposite corner.

Flipping works differently — instead of rotating around a center point, the canvas context is mirrored along one axis using a negative scale transform. Flip Horizontal mirrors content left-to-right, as if you were looking at the image in a mirror held up vertically. Flip Vertical mirrors content top-to-bottom, as if the mirror were held horizontally underneath the image.

Does Rotating or Flipping Reduce Image Quality?

Rotating by exact 90-degree increments (90°, 180°, 270°) does not blur or distort the image, because every pixel simply moves to a new position without any resampling or interpolation needed. The pixel data itself is unchanged — only its arrangement on the canvas changes. The one place quality can be affected is if the final image is saved in a lossy format like JPEG, where re-encoding always applies some compression. This tool downloads as PNG by default, which uses lossless compression, so repeated rotations and flips won't introduce visible quality loss.

Rotate vs. Flip: Which One Do You Need?

If your image is sideways or upside down and you just need it to display the right way up, you want to rotate it — choose 90° left, 90° right, or 180° depending on how far off it is. If your image looks correct in orientation but you need a mirrored version — for example, to match the direction a design element is facing, or to correct text that reads backward because of a scanning issue — you want to flip it instead. Many editing tasks only need one of the two, but this tool lets you combine them freely if a situation calls for both.
