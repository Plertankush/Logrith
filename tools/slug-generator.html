Frequently Asked Questions

What is a URL slug?

A URL slug is the part of a URL that identifies a specific page in human-readable form. For example, in "https://example.com/how-to-build-a-website", the slug is "how-to-build-a-website". Good slugs are descriptive, lowercase, and use hyphens between words.

Should I use hyphens or underscores in slugs?

Google recommends hyphens (-) over underscores (_) for URL slugs. Google treats hyphens as word separators but treats underscores as word joiners. "free-tools" = two words; "free_tools" = one compound word in search engine interpretation.

What are stop words in slug generation?

Stop words are common words (a, an, the, in, on, at, for, of, etc.) that add little SEO value to URLs. Removing them keeps slugs shorter and more keyword-focused. E.g., "the best tools for the web" → "best-tools-web".

Does this tool handle special characters and accents?

Yes, accented characters (like é, ñ, ü) are converted to their closest plain ASCII equivalent, and symbols, punctuation, and other non-alphanumeric characters are stripped out automatically.

How long should a URL slug be?

Shorter slugs that clearly describe the page content tend to work best for both readability and SEO — generally under 5-6 words is a reasonable target, though there's no strict limit.

Is my text sent to a server when generating a slug?

No, the conversion happens instantly in your browser using JavaScript. Nothing you type is transmitted anywhere.

What Makes a Good URL Slug

  • Lowercase only. URLs are technically case-sensitive, and mixing cases creates inconsistency and potential duplicate-content issues if the same page is linked with different capitalization.
  • Hyphens between words, not underscores. Search engines, including Google, treat hyphens as word separators but treat underscores as joining words together into one token, which can hurt how individual keywords in the slug are interpreted.
  • No special characters. Spaces, punctuation, and symbols like &, %, or # either break URLs outright or get encoded into unreadable character sequences, so they should be stripped or replaced.
  • Short and descriptive. A slug that clearly reflects the page's topic in a few words is more useful to both users and search engines than an overly long, literal copy of the full title.

Why Hyphens Instead of Underscores

This distinction trips a lot of people up because both hyphens and underscores look like reasonable word separators visually. The difference matters specifically because of how Google's indexing treats them: a hyphen in "free-tools" is read as two separate words, "free" and "tools," both of which can independently match search queries. An underscore in "free_tools," by contrast, is treated by Google as a single compound token, "free_tools," which won't match a search for just "free" or just "tools" nearly as effectively. For this reason, hyphens have become the standard convention for SEO-friendly URL slugs, and this tool defaults to hyphen separation.

Why Remove Stop Words

Stop words are short, extremely common words — like "a," "the," "in," "on," "for," and "of" — that carry little search relevance on their own. Including them in a slug makes the URL longer without adding meaningful keyword value. For example, "the-best-tools-for-the-web" becomes the more concise "best-tools-web" with stop words removed, keeping the same core meaning in a shorter, more keyword-dense URL. This isn't mandatory — some slugs read more naturally with stop words kept in — but it's a commonly used optimization for content-heavy sites publishing many articles or product pages.

Handling Accented and Special Characters

Titles in many languages include accented characters — é, ñ, ü, and similar — which, while valid in page titles, cause problems if left as-is in a URL, since they either break certain systems or get encoded into long percent-escaped sequences that look messy in a browser address bar. This tool automatically converts accented characters to their closest plain ASCII equivalent (é becomes e, ñ becomes n, and so on), keeping the resulting slug clean and universally compatible across browsers, servers, and content management systems.

Why Slugs Matter for SEO

Search engines use several signals to understand what a page is about, and the URL itself is one of them — a weaker signal than the page title or heading tags, but a signal nonetheless. A slug like "best-budget-laptops-2024" tells both users and search engines what the page covers before anyone even clicks through, which can improve click-through rates from search results and makes shared links more trustworthy-looking. Beyond search engines, clean slugs also simply look better and build more trust when shared on social media, in emails, or pasted into a chat, compared to a URL full of random characters, numbers, or encoded spaces.
