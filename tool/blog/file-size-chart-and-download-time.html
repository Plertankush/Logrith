File Size Chart and Download Time: How Long Will It Take?

File sizes are measured in bytes and connection speeds in bits, which is why a 100 Mbps plan does not download 100 MB in a second. This guide gives the chart and the one calculation you need.

File Size Chart

Each unit is 1,000 times the one before it in the decimal system used by storage makers and network speeds. Operating systems often use 1,024 instead, which is why the two systems give slightly different numbers.

UnitDecimal (1,000)Binary (1,024)
1 KB1,000 bytes1,024 bytes
1 MB1,000 KB1,024 KB
1 GB1,000 MB1,024 MB
1 TB1,000 GB1,024 GB

So 1 GB is 1,000 MB in the decimal system and 1,024 MB in the binary one. The File Size Converter shows both at once. For the reason behind the two systems, read our file size converter guide.

Megabits Are Not Megabytes

Internet speeds are sold in megabits per second (Mbps, with a small b). File sizes are shown in megabytes (MB, with a capital B). One byte has 8 bits, so divide Mbps by 8 to get MB per second.

  • 50 Mbps is about 6.25 MB per second.
  • 100 Mbps is about 12.5 MB per second.
  • 500 Mbps is about 62.5 MB per second.

How to Estimate Download Time

Use this formula: time in seconds = file size in MB × 8 ÷ speed in Mbps. For a 1 GB (1,000 MB) file on a 50 Mbps connection that is 1,000 × 8 ÷ 50 = 160 seconds, or about 2 minutes 40 seconds.

Real downloads are usually slower than this. Wi-Fi quality, server speed, other devices on the network and protocol overhead all take a share, so treat the figures as a floor, not a promise.

What Common Files Weigh

Sizes vary a lot with quality and settings, so these are rough guides only.

  • A text document is usually a few tens of KB.
  • A phone photo is typically a few MB, depending on the camera and format.
  • A 3.5 minute song as MP3 is about 3 MB at 128 kbps and about 8 MB at 320 kbps.
  • Video is the largest everyday file. Length and resolution decide the size, from tens of MB for a short clip to many GB for a long film.

When a File Is Too Large

Forms, email and messaging apps set upload limits, and the number is often given in MB. If a file is over the limit, shrink it first. Use the Image Compressor for photos and the PDF Compressor for documents, then check the new size in the converter.

Common Mistakes

  • Mixing bits and bytes. This makes a download look eight times faster than it is.
  • Expecting the full advertised speed. Quoted speeds are the maximum, not the average.
  • Mixing 1,000 and 1,024. It explains why a drive shows less space than its label.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many MB are in a GB?

1 GB is 1,000 MB in the decimal system used by storage makers and 1,024 MB in the binary system used by many operating systems.

How do I convert Mbps to MB per second?

Divide the speed in Mbps by 8. For example, 100 Mbps is about 12.5 MB per second.

How long does it take to download 1 GB?

At 100 Mbps the best case is about 1 minute 20 seconds, and at 50 Mbps about 2 minutes 40 seconds. Real speeds are usually lower.

Why is my download slower than my plan?

The plan speed is a maximum. Wi-Fi quality, the server, other devices and protocol overhead all reduce what you actually get.

What is the difference between MB and Mb?

MB with a capital B means megabytes and Mb with a small b means megabits. One megabyte is 8 megabits.

Conclusion

Remember two things: divide Mbps by 8 to get MB per second, and expect real downloads to be slower than the formula. For everything else, convert the sizes with a tool.
