Time Zones and Daylight Saving: How to Convert Time Correctly

Converting a time sounds easy until half-hour offsets and daylight saving enter the picture. This guide explains the rules in plain language and gives a method that avoids the usual mistakes.

UTC and Offsets

Every time zone is described by its difference from UTC, Coordinated Universal Time. India Standard Time is UTC+5:30, which means its clocks are five and a half hours ahead of UTC. New York is UTC−5 in winter and UTC−4 in summer. UTC itself does not change with the seasons, which makes it a safe reference point.

Not all offsets are whole hours. India is UTC+5:30, Nepal is UTC+5:45 and parts of Australia use UTC+9:30. A converter that only adds whole hours will be wrong for these places.

Zone Names Versus Abbreviations

Abbreviations such as IST or CST are ambiguous. IST can mean India, Ireland or Israel, and CST can mean North American Central time or China time. Software uses names such as Asia/Kolkata or America/New_York from the IANA time zone database, which are unambiguous and carry the daylight saving rules for each place.

How Daylight Saving Time Works

Some countries move their clocks forward in spring and back in autumn to shift daylight into the evening. The dates differ by region.

RegionClocks go forwardClocks go back
United States and Canada (most areas)Second Sunday in MarchFirst Sunday in November
European Union and United KingdomLast Sunday in MarchLast Sunday in October
India, Japan and ChinaNo daylight savingNo daylight saving

Because the US and Europe change on different dates, the gap between them is different for a few weeks every year. A meeting that is at the same local time in both places can suddenly be an hour off.

A Simple Method for Converting a Time

  1. Write the time and the date in the source zone, not just the time.

  2. Convert it to UTC by subtracting the source offset on that date.

  3. Add the target offset on that same date to get the local time.

  4. Check whether the result moved to the previous or next day.

Example. 9:00 AM in India (UTC+5:30) is 03:30 UTC. In New York that is 10:30 PM the previous day in winter (UTC−5) and 11:30 PM the previous day in summer (UTC−4). The Time Zone Converter does these steps for you and handles daylight saving automatically.

Scheduling Across Time Zones

  • Share the time in UTC or in each person’s zone, with the date, so nobody has to calculate.
  • Look for the overlap of normal working hours. India and the UK share a short morning overlap, while India and the US West Coast have very little.
  • Check the dates around March and late October to November, when daylight saving changes.
  • Keep a world clock open for the cities you work with most.

Common Mistakes

  • Forgetting the date. A time can fall on the previous or next day in another zone.
  • Assuming a fixed gap. The difference between two places can change with daylight saving.
  • Using ambiguous abbreviations such as IST or CST without a place name.
  • Assuming whole-hour offsets. Several large regions use 30 or 45 minute offsets.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I convert IST to EST?

India (IST) is UTC+5:30 and US Eastern is UTC-5 in winter, so IST is 10 hours 30 minutes ahead. In summer, when Eastern is UTC-4, the gap is 9 hours 30 minutes.

Why is India UTC+5:30?

India uses a single time zone based on the 82.5 degrees east meridian, which is five and a half hours ahead of UTC. India does not use daylight saving time.

Which countries do not use daylight saving time?

Many do not, including India, Japan and China. Most of the world near the equator also stays on one time all year.

When does daylight saving start?

In the US and Canada it starts on the second Sunday in March and ends on the first Sunday in November. In the EU and UK it starts on the last Sunday in March and ends on the last Sunday in October.

What is the best way to schedule a meeting across time zones?

Pick a time that falls in working hours for everyone, state it with the date and with each person's zone or in UTC, and check the dates when daylight saving changes.

Conclusion

Time zones are simple once you work from UTC and always include the date. Use the method above for understanding and a converter for speed.
