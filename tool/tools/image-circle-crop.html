Frequently Asked Questions

How do I crop a picture into a circle?

Upload your image, drag it until the part you want sits inside the circle, adjust the zoom, then click Download. The result is a square image with a circular picture and transparent corners.

Will the circle crop have a transparent background?

Yes, when you save as PNG or WEBP. The area outside the circle is fully transparent. JPG cannot store transparency, so the tool fills the corners with the background color you choose.

Can I set the exact size of the circle?

Yes. Type any size from 32 to 4096 pixels or use the 128, 256, 512 and 1024 presets. The output is always a square with the circle touching all four edges.

Is my photo uploaded to a server?

No. The image is drawn onto a canvas inside your browser and the file is created on your own device. Nothing is sent to Logrith or anyone else.

Why does my circular image look square on some sites?

A transparent PNG only looks round if the site keeps transparency. Some platforms flatten images to a white or black background, and others apply their own round mask to the whole square. Upload the square result and let the platform round it, or use PNG where transparency is supported.

Can I make an oval or a rounded square instead?

This tool makes perfect circles. For a rounded square or any custom corner shape use the Round Image Corners tool linked below.

What is the best size for a profile picture?

Most platforms display profile pictures between 100 and 400 pixels, but they store larger versions. A 512 or 1024 pixel export stays sharp on high-density screens and is small enough to upload quickly.

Round images are used everywhere people are represented on screen: profile pictures, team pages, comment avatars, speaker photos, author boxes, chat apps and product testimonials. Many of those places expect a square upload and apply a circular mask themselves. Others, such as a website you build yourself, a slide deck or a printed badge, need the circle to be part of the file. This tool covers both cases because it always exports a square image with the circle touching every edge.

How the circle crop works

When you upload a picture, the shorter side of the image is scaled to fill the circle exactly, so no empty gap appears at zoom 100%. Dragging moves the picture under the circle and zooming enlarges it up to four times. The tool stops you from dragging so far that an empty edge would show inside the circle.

On export, the browser draws the photo onto a square canvas, clips it with a circular path and, for PNG and WEBP, leaves the corners transparent. Because the crop is made from the original pixels at the size you choose, a large source photo stays sharp. A small source stretched to a much larger output will look soft, so match the output size to what you actually need.

Choosing PNG, WEBP or JPG

  • PNG keeps full quality and a transparent background. It is the safest choice for avatars and logos.
  • WEBP also keeps transparency and is usually much smaller than PNG, which helps pages load faster. All current browsers support it.
  • JPG gives the smallest files for photographs but cannot be transparent. The tool fills the area outside the circle with the background color you pick, white by default.

Adding a ring around the circle

A thin ring can separate a profile photo from a busy or similar-colored background. Set a width in pixels and a color, and the ring is drawn on the inside edge of the circle so the overall size stays exactly what you chose. A white ring suits dark backgrounds, and a dark or brand-colored ring suits light ones. Leave the width at zero for a plain circle.

Tips for a better profile picture

Keep the face near the center of the circle, because the corners of the original photo are cut away. Leave a little space above the head so the crop does not feel tight. Use a photo with soft, even light and a simple background; small avatars show little detail, so a clear face matters more than a detailed scene. Zoom in slightly rather than using a small face in a wide photo, since avatars are often viewed at only a few dozen pixels.

Why a circle can still look square

If your circular PNG shows a white or black square after uploading, the platform is flattening transparency. Some systems convert every upload to JPG. In that case, upload the square version and let the service apply its own mask, or choose the background color that matches the page. If the image looks fine in the preview here but not on the site, the cause is almost always how that site handles transparency, not the file.

Privacy

Everything happens in your browser. The file you choose is read locally, drawn to a canvas and saved locally. Logrith never receives the image, which makes this a sensible choice for personal photos, ID-style pictures and client work you are not allowed to share.

Circle crop vs rounded corners

A circle is one specific shape: every point on the edge is the same distance from the center. A rounded rectangle keeps straight sides and only softens the corners. If you want a square image with gently rounded corners, an app-icon look or a pill shape, use the Round Image Corners tool, where you set the radius yourself and can give each corner a different value.
