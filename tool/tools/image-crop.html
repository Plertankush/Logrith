Frequently Asked Questions

How do I crop an image online?

Upload your image, then drag on the preview to select the crop area. You can also type exact pixel coordinates in the X, Y, Width, Height fields. Click Crop Image to apply.

Can I crop to a specific aspect ratio?

Yes! Use the preset buttons (1:1, 16:9, 4:3, 9:16) to automatically set a crop area with the correct proportions centered on the image.

Will cropping affect image quality?

No, cropping only removes the parts outside your selection. The remaining area retains its full original quality.

Is my image uploaded to a server?

No, all cropping happens locally using your browser's canvas feature. Your image never leaves your device during the process.

What aspect ratios are available?

Square (1:1), widescreen (16:9), standard (4:3), and portrait/story (9:16) presets are built in, or you can enter exact X, Y, width, and height values for a fully custom crop area.

What format does the cropped image download as?

Cropped images download as PNG files by default, which preserves full image quality and supports transparency if your original image had any.

Using Aspect Ratio Presets

Different platforms and use cases call for different image proportions. A square crop (1:1) works well for profile pictures and most social media grid posts. Widescreen (16:9) matches video thumbnails and presentation slides. Standard (4:3) is common for traditional photo prints and some older display formats. Portrait (9:16) matches the vertical format used by Stories and Reels on most social platforms. Selecting a preset automatically draws a correctly proportioned crop box centered on your image, which you can then drag to reposition without needing to calculate the dimensions yourself.

How Cropping Works

When you crop an image, the tool draws only the selected rectangular region onto a new HTML5 canvas, discarding everything outside that area. Because this process simply copies existing pixel data to a new, smaller canvas rather than resampling or interpolating anything, the pixels that remain inside your crop area keep their exact original quality — there's no blurring, no added compression artifacts, and no loss of sharpness from the crop operation itself.

Cropping vs. Resizing: What's the Difference?

These two operations are often confused but do different things. Cropping removes part of the image, keeping the remaining section at its original resolution — you end up with a smaller image because you've cut away pixels you didn't want. Resizing instead keeps the entire image but scales every pixel up or down to fit new dimensions, which can affect sharpness if scaling up significantly. If your goal is to remove unwanted background or focus on a subject, crop. If your goal is to make the whole image smaller or larger in file size or display dimensions without losing any of the content, use Logrith's Image Resize tool instead. The two are often used together — crop first to frame the subject, then resize to the exact dimensions a platform requires.

Getting Precise Crops for Platform Requirements

Many platforms specify exact pixel dimensions or aspect ratios for images — a profile photo might need to be square, a banner image might need a specific width-to-height ratio, and a thumbnail might have both minimum and maximum size requirements. When you know the exact dimensions required, entering precise X, Y, width, and height values rather than dragging freehand ensures the crop matches those requirements exactly, avoiding a platform rejecting or auto-cropping your upload in a way you didn't intend.
