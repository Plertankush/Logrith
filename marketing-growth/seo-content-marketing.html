SEO & Content Marketing

How search engines evaluate pages, how to plan and write content that earns traffic, and how to build a site Google and readers trust.

By Plert Ankush

Overview

Search engine optimisation is the practice of making your pages easy for search engines to understand and genuinely useful to the people who find them. The two goals are the same in the long run: engines reward pages that satisfy readers.

Content marketing is the publishing side. You answer real questions in depth, build a library over time and let search bring a steady audience. It is slower than ads, but the work compounds.

What this section covers

  • Keyword research: intent, difficulty and realistic targets
  • On-page basics: titles, descriptions, headings, internal links, canonical tags
  • Technical SEO: crawlability, sitemaps, speed and structured data
  • Writing content that is original and useful rather than padded
  • E-E-A-T: experience, expertise, authoritativeness, trust
  • Measuring progress with Search Console and analytics

Core concepts

Search intent

What the searcher actually wants: an answer, a comparison, a tool or a purchase. Content must match it.

Canonical tag

A tag telling search engines which URL is the preferred version of a page, which prevents duplicate-content confusion.

Internal linking

Links between your own pages, which help readers and crawlers discover and understand related content.

Thin content

Pages that add little unique value. Expanding them with real substance is better than hiding them.

Where to start

Choose ten questions your audience genuinely asks, write one thorough answer for the most common and publish it. Then check Search Console after a few weeks to see which queries it starts to appear for.
