Who I Am

I'm Plert Ankush, a solo founder and builder — my legal name is Ankush Maurya. I use the same identity, Plert Ankush, on every platform, so anyone who finds me on one platform can trust it's genuinely the same person everywhere else.

I'm not a traditional software engineer with a formal coding background. I'm a founder who learned to build by doing, and who now builds entire products using AI as my core development process — a practice often called "vibecoding." Logrith, every tool on it, every line of code behind it, and every piece of content on this site, including this page, was built this way: through direction, iteration, and collaboration with AI systems, not hand-written line by line in the traditional sense.

I think this is worth stating plainly rather than hiding. Logrith is a real, working, free product used by real people — and it was built by one person directing AI tools to do it, start to finish. I believe this is an honest and increasingly common way products get built today, and I'd rather be transparent about it than pretend otherwise.

Where I Am Today

I started Logrith as a library of free browser-based tools. The tools found real users, but growth stalled, so I am now building Logrith into a publication about AI, developer and creator tools, with the tools kept as a separate section. I am learning in public, improving it step by step and fixing what doesn't work.

Along the way, I've become interested in the broader world of technology, AI, software, startups, and the internet. Logrith is one of the places where I put that curiosity into practice.

How I Think About Building

I prefer building things that have a clear purpose rather than adding complexity just to make a product look impressive. A useful tool can be simple. A good product should make someone's task easier, faster, or less frustrating.

I also believe transparency matters. That is why I openly explain how Logrith is built, how AI fits into my workflow, and what the project is trying to achieve instead of presenting a polished story that hides the process behind it.

Learning While Building

Building Logrith has also become a way for me to learn. Every new feature brings another problem to understand — from product decisions and interface design to code, performance, SEO, content, deployment, and maintenance.

I don't see not knowing everything as a reason to stop building. I see it as part of the process. Learn what is needed, build it, test it, fix it, and keep moving.

What's Next

The plan is to keep publishing in-depth guides across AI & Developer Tools and Build & Ship, update them as things change, and keep the free tools useful. The goal is not to chase every trend, but to publish practical material that people can actually use.

Beyond Logrith, I want to keep exploring technology, AI, software, and new ideas. This founder page is not meant to be a finished biography — it is a snapshot of the person building, learning, and figuring things out along the way.

Why I Built Logrith

I kept running into the same frustration: simple digital tasks — compressing an image, converting a file, calculating something quick — were locked behind subscriptions, intrusive ads, or sites that uploaded your files to unknown servers just to do something that could run entirely in a browser. It felt unnecessary and, often, exploitative of people who just needed something simple done.

That frustration became the tools section of Logrith: genuinely free, fast, private tools that don't ask for your email, your file uploads, or your money to do basic digital work. The same principle now guides the writing: clear, practical explanations without hype, paywalls or padding.

My long-term goal isn't to compete head-on with giant, well-funded publishers. It's to build something genuinely useful, keep it honest and sustainable through simple, non-intrusive advertising, and let the quality of the work speak for itself.

A note on how this site is built: Logrith's tools, articles and SEO are developed with AI assistance as a core part of the process, directed and reviewed by me. I'm the founder, strategist, and editor — AI is the hands-on builder. I think that's a fair and increasingly normal way to describe how modern solo-founder products actually get made, and I'd rather say so clearly than let anyone assume otherwise.
