SaaS & Product Building

How to plan, build and ship a software-as-a-service product: scoping, pricing, onboarding and keeping customers.

By Plert Ankush

Overview

Software-as-a-service sells ongoing access rather than a one-time copy. That changes the economics: revenue arrives monthly, so retention matters as much as acquisition, and a product that people stop using will eventually stop paying.

Building one well means scoping tightly, shipping early, listening to usage and pricing in a way that matches the value delivered. This sub-topic covers each stage with an emphasis on small teams and solo founders.

What this section covers

  • Scoping a first version you can ship in weeks
  • Choosing a stack: boring technology and when it is the right call
  • Pricing models: flat, tiered, usage-based and how to test them
  • Onboarding that gets a new user to value quickly
  • Measuring retention, churn and activation
  • Support, documentation and feedback loops

Core concepts

MRR

Monthly recurring revenue: the predictable subscription revenue you expect each month.

Churn

The share of customers or revenue lost over a period. Small differences compound heavily over time.

Activation

The point at which a new user first experiences the core value of the product.

Usage-based pricing

Charging according to consumption, which aligns cost with value but makes revenue less predictable.

Where to start

Write the one sentence describing the outcome your product delivers, list the fewest features needed to deliver it and cut everything else from version one. Ship it to a handful of users and watch what they do.
