Business Models, Finance & Operations

Understand how businesses make money: revenue models, margins, cash flow, pricing maths and running operations efficiently.

By Plert Ankush

Overview

A business model is the answer to a simple question: who pays, for what, and why does the maths work? Many struggling companies have a product people like but economics that do not add up. Understanding margins, cash flow and unit economics early prevents that.

Operations is the quieter half: the processes, tools and habits that let you deliver consistently as you grow. This sub-topic pairs the concepts with calculators for the arithmetic founders do constantly.

What this section covers

  • Common business models: subscription, marketplace, services, ads, licensing
  • Gross margin, net margin and why they differ
  • Cash flow versus profit and how companies run out of money
  • Pricing maths: discounts, taxes, fees and break-even
  • Loans, interest and the real cost of borrowing
  • Simple operating routines for small teams

Core concepts

Unit economics

The revenue and cost attached to a single customer or order, used to judge whether growth is healthy.

Gross margin

Revenue minus the direct cost of delivering it, expressed as a share of revenue.

Cash flow

The timing of money moving in and out. A profitable business can still fail if cash arrives too late.

Break-even point

The volume at which revenue covers all costs.

Where to start

Take your most important product or service and write one line of unit economics: what a customer pays, what it costs you to serve them and what is left. If you cannot fill that line in, that is the first thing to find out.
