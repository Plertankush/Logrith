Startup Ideas & Validation

How to generate startup ideas, test demand before building and decide whether an idea deserves your time and money.

By Plert Ankush

Overview

A good idea is a problem plus a person who feels it strongly enough to act. Validation is the work of checking both before you invest months of effort. It is cheaper to be disproved by ten conversations than by a finished product nobody opens.

The most reliable early signals are behavioural: someone gives you time, a deposit, a signed intent or a repeat visit. Compliments, likes and polite interest are weak signals and should be treated as such.

What this section covers

  • Finding problems worth solving: your own work, communities and complaints
  • Customer interviews: questions that avoid leading people
  • Landing-page and waitlist tests and how to read the results
  • Sizing a market without inflated numbers
  • Competitor research: what existing alternatives teach you
  • Deciding when to build, pivot or stop

Core concepts

Problem-solution fit

Evidence that a specific group has a real problem and that your approach addresses it.

Minimum viable product

The smallest version that lets you learn whether people want the outcome. It is a learning tool, not a low-quality launch.

Smoke test

A cheap test, such as a page describing a product that does not yet exist, to measure real interest.

Willingness to pay

Whether people will exchange money, time or effort for the solution, which is stronger evidence than agreement.

Where to start

Write down one problem, name who has it and find five of those people to talk to this week. Ask about the last time the problem happened and what they did, not whether they would like your idea.
